Your weekly injection guide.

General instructions for patients. Your prescription and your provider's directions always come first.

Where to inject

  • Abdomen: at least 2 inches from your belly button.
  • Thigh: upper outer thigh, between knee and groin.
  • Back of upper arm: if someone can help you.

Change sites each week to reduce skin irritation.

How to inject

  1. Wash your hands and clean the site with an alcohol wipe.
  2. Hold the syringe like a dart and gently pinch your skin.
  3. Insert the needle with a quick, straight motion.
  4. Push the plunger slowly until all the medicine is delivered.
  5. Pull the needle out the same way it went in.
  6. Put the needle in a sharps container. Refrigerate unused syringes.

Quick tips

  • Refrigerate your medication. Don't freeze it.
  • Never reuse needles.
  • Keep track of your weekly dose.
  • Ask your provider before changing any dose.

Important safety information

Medical director to approve all safety text

GLP-1 medications aren't right for everyone. Serious risks can include thyroid tumors, pancreatitis, gallbladder problems, kidney problems and low blood sugar. Don't use them if you are pregnant, or if you or your family have a history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2. Common side effects include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea and constipation.

Compounded medications are not FDA-approved and may differ in risks, benefits and side effects from approved products. A licensed provider performs a telehealth exam to determine eligibility, and not all patients qualify. Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly are the only US manufacturers of the approved semaglutide and tirzepatide products, and they do not supply those medications for compounding.
