How to check a peptide provider

Six questions that help separate a medical practice from a research-chemical site.

Why it's confusing

Peptide rules are changing. FDA has updated its compounding lists, and an advisory committee recommended six peptides for its bulk substances list in 2026. FDA has also sent warning letters to peptide sellers.

Six questions to ask

  1. Does a licensed provider evaluate me before prescribing?
  2. Who is that provider, and is the license valid in my state?
  3. Which licensed pharmacy prepares it?
  4. Is it labeled for human use, or "research use only"?
  5. What does the human evidence show, including what isn't known?
  6. Who follows up with me afterward?

Red flags

  • No medical evaluation or prescription
  • Promises of guaranteed results
  • Vague answers about where the product comes from
