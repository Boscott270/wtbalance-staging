What it is

Semaglutide acts like GLP-1, a natural hormone involved in appetite and blood sugar. It is taken by injection once a week. Your program includes B12.

Side effects and risks

Common side effects include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea and constipation. These are often most noticeable when starting or raising a dose. Tell your provider about any symptoms, and get urgent care for severe stomach pain, signs of an allergic reaction, or trouble breathing.

Important safety information

GLP-1 medications aren't right for everyone. Serious risks can include thyroid tumors, pancreatitis, gallbladder problems, kidney problems and low blood sugar. The risk of low blood sugar is higher if you also take insulin or a sulfonylurea. Semaglutide may worsen diabetic eye disease (retinopathy). Tirzepatide can make birth control pills less effective. Don't use GLP-1 medications if you are pregnant, or if you or your family have a history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2. Compounded medications are not FDA-approved and may differ in risks, benefits and side effects from approved products.
